# How Brian reviews a scene (audit lens for Space Snack Quest)

Brian is the designer. He playtests with his young kid on an iPad held sideways. Judge every moment the way he does. These come from his actual past feedback.

## Story and clarity (like a kids' book)
- Stakes before problems. Characters are introduced before they matter. Setups pay off. Simple words.
- Every alert or event has a visible story cause. Nothing happens arbitrarily or repeats without a reason.
- Nothing pops up without a player trigger. Reveals come from inspecting things. Only clear cutscenes play on their own.
- Make the key thing OBVIOUS to a young kid, e.g. the paperwork's fate, the snack pyramid, the balloon rising. If a kid could miss it, it fails.
- Emotional reactions fit the moment, e.g. curious, not sad, when peeking at the robots.
- Nothing Squatch picks up just disappears; he holds it up briefly or puts it somewhere visible.
- Players should never feel forced into a spot. Use a cutscene rather than an invisible wall or an arbitrary rule.

## Continuity
- No teleports. Characters walk to where they end up.
- Rooms, furniture, props, outfits and characters stay consistent with the locked art and the existing home room layout.
- Don't invent new rooms; scenes happen in known rooms.
- Adults (manager, workers, guests) are clearly taller than Squatch and Mops. Guests are a varied mix of alien types in the simple rounded thick-outline style.
- Mops mops with his beard; there's no mop prop anywhere. 'MY GARDIANS' is an intentional kid spelling.

## Playability and feel
- Discovery comes from subtle in-world nudges, not tutorial text. Keep UI clutter low. Goals disappear once done. NEXT gently pulses after a few idle seconds.
- Close-up "cut-ins" work like the original Sneaky Sasquatch oven, cooler and eating views. A long furry arm's paw follows the finger, dropped items snap, the room shows blurred behind, and a white X zooms back out.
- Touch targets are big enough for a kid's finger. Every tap gives a satisfying response.
- Fair challenge: a player who watches and waits can succeed. Never 0 out of 5.
- He wants it FUN moment to moment: humor, juicy reactions, little surprises and Easter eggs.

## Look
- Clean, bright, readable on an iPad in landscape. No stray sprites, overlaps, z-order mistakes, frame jumps or dark tints during normal play.
- Characters stand on the floor, not floating, and don't pass through each other.

## Audio
- Music at times, not constant: Joe's warm theme for heart moments, a sneaky/chase theme, a journey theme, short stings, and sound effects for noisy actions.

## Rules the auditor must respect
- New on-screen words are pending until Brian OKs them, so propose them without adding them live.
- Difficulty, timer, price and range changes are recommendations with a reason, not changes.
